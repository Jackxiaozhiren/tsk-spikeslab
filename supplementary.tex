\section{Slab Variance Sensitivity (Coefficient-Level SSVS)}

Table~\ref{tab:tau2} reports the coefficient-level SSVS ablation on the Energy-Cooling target (10 splits, $\pi=0.5$, $R=5$ rules). Varying the slab variance $\tau^2$ from $0.1$ to $100$ (three orders of magnitude) spans the full sparsity--accuracy trade-off: a tight slab ($\tau^2=0.1$) over-shrinks the coefficients and collapses accuracy to $R^2=-5.42$, whereas a diffuse slab ($\tau^2=100$) merely recovers the dense baseline ($R^2=0.92$ versus $0.94$ for TSK-LS) while pruning nothing. No $\tau^2$ setting beats the dense baseline.

\section{Irrelevant-Feature Ablation}

Table~\ref{tab:noise} reports the irrelevant-feature regime on the Energy-Cooling target: up to 30 standard-normal noise features are appended to the $d=8$ inputs. The dense TSK-LS degrades only modestly (from $R^2=0.93$ to $0.88$), and neither rule-level (TSK-SpikeSlab-Gibbs) nor coefficient-level (TSK-SSVS) sparsity improves upon it. Correct fuzzy $c$-means already provides robustness to irrelevant features, so sparsity offers no free lunch on this low-dimensional domain.
